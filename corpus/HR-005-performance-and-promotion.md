# HR-005 — Performance Management and Promotion

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Scope
This document describes how performance is assessed at Meridian, the review calendar, the rating scale, how promotions are decided, what happens when performance falls short, and how internal transfers work.

### 1.2 Related Documents
Levels and level expectations are in ORG-002. The compensation consequences of a rating are in HR-002 §4.2 and §5.2.

## 2. Review Calendar

### 2.1 Two Cycles Per Year
Meridian runs two review cycles per year.

| Cycle | Period assessed | Written reviews due | Ratings finalized | Conversations held |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | 1 January – 30 June | 15 July | 31 July | August |
| H2 | 1 July – 31 December | 15 January | 31 January | February |

### 2.2 Relationship to Compensation
Only the **H2 rating** feeds the annual compensation review, which runs in February and March with changes effective 1 April (HR-002 §4.1). The H1 rating does not drive a merit increase; it exists to give a mid-year signal and to establish the promotion track record described in §6.2.

### 2.3 Eligibility to Be Rated
An employee must have been in role for at least 90 days at the end of the review period to receive a rating. Employees below that threshold receive a "Too New to Rate" status, which is not a rating and does not count toward or against promotion eligibility.

## 3. Continuous Feedback

### 3.1 Expectation
Formal cycles document performance; they do not discover it. A rating in a written review should never be the first time an employee hears the underlying assessment. This is the "Say the Uncomfortable Number" value in ORG-001 §4.2 applied to people management.

### 3.2 One-to-Ones
Managers hold a scheduled one-to-one with each direct report at least every two weeks. These are the employee's meeting; the manager's role is to listen, unblock, and give direct feedback.

### 3.3 Peer Feedback
Each cycle, an employee nominates 3 to 5 peers for written feedback, and the manager may add up to 2 more. Peer feedback is shared with the employee in summarized, non-anonymized form — Meridian does not run anonymous peer review, on the grounds that unattributable criticism is not actionable.

## 4. Rating Scale

### 4.1 The Five Ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Exceptional | Consistently delivered beyond the scope of the level; impact visible well outside the team |
| Strong | Exceeded expectations for the level in most areas |
| Solid | Met the expectations of the level |
| Developing | Fell short in one or more significant areas; a specific improvement plan is agreed |
| Not Meeting | Substantially below the expectations of the level; formal performance improvement plan initiated (§5) |

### 4.2 No Forced Distribution
Meridian does not apply a forced curve. Calibration sessions (§4.3) exist to align standards across managers, not to fit ratings to a quota.

### 4.3 Calibration
Before ratings are finalized, managers within a function meet to compare proposed ratings against level expectations. A rating may be changed in calibration; where it is, the manager must convey the reasoning to the employee in the review conversation, not attribute it to an anonymous committee.

### 4.4 "Solid" Is a Good Rating
An employee rated Solid is meeting the expectations of their level and is in good standing. Most employees are rated Solid in most cycles. Solid ratings receive merit increases under HR-002 §4.2 and are eligible for full bonus payout under HR-002 §5.2.

## 5. Performance Improvement

### 5.1 When a Plan Is Used
A performance improvement plan is initiated when an employee is rated Not Meeting, or when performance deteriorates materially between cycles such that waiting for the next cycle would not serve the employee.

### 5.2 Duration and Structure
A plan runs for **60 calendar days**. It states specific, observable outcomes, the support Meridian will provide, and a weekly check-in cadence. It is written by the manager, reviewed by People Operations before delivery, and delivered in person or by video, never by message alone.

### 5.3 Outcomes
At the end of the plan period one of three things happens: the plan is closed successfully; the plan is extended once by up to 30 days where progress is real but incomplete; or employment ends under HR-007 §3.

### 5.4 During a Plan
An employee on an active plan is not eligible for promotion (§6.2), for internal transfer (§7.3), or for a retention offer. They remain eligible for all leave under HR-004 and all benefits under HR-003. A plan does not affect on-call rotation participation.

## 6. Promotion

### 6.1 What a Promotion Is
A promotion is a move to a higher level in ORG-002 §3, reflecting that the employee has been operating at that higher level, not that they are expected to grow into it. Meridian promotes on demonstrated scope, not on potential.

### 6.2 Eligibility Requirements
To be put forward for promotion an employee must satisfy all of the following:

1. A rating of **Strong or Exceptional in two consecutive completed cycles**, the most recent of which must be the cycle immediately preceding the promotion round.
2. At least **12 months in the current level**, except for promotion from IC1 to IC2, which requires 6 months.
3. Not on an active performance improvement plan (§5.4) at any point during the two qualifying cycles.
4. Manager and skip-level manager both supporting the case in writing.

A lateral track change under ORG-002 §2.4 does not reset the 12-month clock in requirement 2. A change of manager does not reset it either; the incoming manager inherits the record.

### 6.3 Promotion Rounds
Promotions are decided twice a year, immediately after each rating cycle finalizes: in **August** (following H1) and in **February** (following H2). Promotion cases are reviewed by a committee composed of the function's M3+ leaders and a People Operations representative.

### 6.4 Effective Date and Compensation
A promotion approved in the February round takes effect 1 April, alongside the annual compensation review (HR-002 §4.1). A promotion approved in the August round takes effect **1 September** and carries an off-cycle base salary increase permitted under HR-002 §4.3. Bonus target changes (HR-002 §5.1) apply prospectively from the effective date and are pro-rated for the plan year.

### 6.5 Promotion to IC6/M3 and Above
Cases at IC6/M3 and above additionally require the sponsorship of a functional executive and are reviewed by the leadership team rather than the function committee.

### 6.6 Unsuccessful Cases
An employee whose case is not approved must be told the specific gap and what closing it looks like. A case may be resubmitted in the next round; there is no waiting period beyond the normal cycle.

## 7. Internal Mobility

### 7.1 Posting
All open roles are posted internally at the same time they are posted externally. Employees may apply directly without informing their manager first; the recruiter informs the manager once the employee reaches the interview stage.

### 7.2 Transfer Timing
An approved internal transfer takes effect no sooner than 30 calendar days after acceptance, to allow handover. The releasing manager may request an extension of up to 30 further days; the extension is granted or refused by People Operations, not by the releasing manager.

### 7.3 Eligibility
An employee must have completed their probationary period (HR-001 §3) and must have been in their current role for at least 12 months to transfer, and must not be on an active performance improvement plan. The 12-month requirement may be waived by People Operations where the transfer serves a business need.

### 7.4 Level and Compensation on Transfer
An internal transfer is normally at the same level with no change in base salary. Where the receiving role is in a different salary zone (HR-002 §3.3) and the employee's work location changes as a result, the zone adjustment applies. A transfer is never a mechanism for promotion; a level increase must go through §6.
