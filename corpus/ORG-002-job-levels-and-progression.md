# ORG-002 — Job Levels and Career Framework

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Scope
This document defines Meridian's job levels, the scope expected at each level, and the equivalence between the individual contributor and management tracks. It is referenced by compensation (HR-002), performance and promotion (HR-005), travel entitlements (FIN-001), and equipment standards (IT-001).

### 1.2 What This Document Does Not Cover
This document does not set salary. Salary bands are maintained in HR-002 §3. It also does not describe the promotion process, which is in HR-005 §6.

## 2. The Two Tracks

### 2.1 Individual Contributor Track
IC1 through IC7. The IC track rewards technical and functional depth. Progression is defined by increasing scope of ambiguity handled, not by headcount managed.

### 2.2 Management Track
M1 through M5. The M track rewards organizational leverage. An M1 typically manages 4–8 people; an M5 is a functional executive.

### 2.3 Track Equivalence
The tracks are equivalent at the following pairings. Where a policy in this handbook states a threshold using one track, the equivalent level on the other track is included.

| IC level | Management level | Typical title |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IC1 | — | Associate |
| IC2 | — | Engineer / Analyst |
| IC3 | — | Senior |
| IC4 | M1 | Staff / Manager |
| IC5 | M2 | Senior Staff / Senior Manager |
| IC6 | M3 | Principal / Director |
| IC7 | M4 | Distinguished / Senior Director |
| — | M5 | Vice President |

Note that IC7 maps to M4, and there is no IC equivalent to M5. Policies that use "M4 and above" as a threshold therefore include IC7 but not IC6.

### 2.4 Track Changes
Moving between tracks at the same level is a lateral move, not a promotion, and does not require promotion committee review. It requires the agreement of both managers and a 30-day notice to the receiving team. A lateral track change does not reset the promotion-eligibility clock described in HR-005 §6.2.

## 3. Level Expectations

### 3.1 IC1 — Associate
Executes well-defined tasks with regular guidance. Impact is measured within a single project. Typically 0–2 years of relevant experience. Meridian hires at IC1 primarily through the university and apprenticeship programs.

### 3.2 IC2 — Engineer / Analyst
Owns well-scoped features end to end. Requires occasional guidance on design. Impact within a team.

### 3.3 IC3 — Senior
Owns ambiguous problems within a team's domain. Sets technical direction for projects. Mentors IC1–IC2. Impact within a team, visible across adjacent teams. IC3 is Meridian's "terminal level" in the sense that an employee may remain at IC3 indefinitely with strong performance ratings and no expectation of further promotion.

### 3.4 IC4 / M1 — Staff / Manager
IC4 owns problems spanning multiple teams and is accountable for outcomes they do not personally implement. M1 manages a single team, owns delivery and the performance of 4–8 reports, and is accountable for hiring within the team.

### 3.5 IC5 / M2 — Senior Staff / Senior Manager
IC5 sets multi-quarter technical direction for a function-sized area and is expected to identify problems nobody has assigned. M2 manages managers or a large single team (9–15) and owns a roadmap area.

### 3.6 IC6 / M3 — Principal / Director
Company-wide scope. IC6 work reshapes how a function operates. M3 owns a sub-function, its budget, and its org design.

### 3.7 IC7 / M4 — Distinguished / Senior Director
Scope extends beyond Meridian: standards, industry positioning, or category definition. Fewer than ten employees hold IC7 or M4.

### 3.8 M5 — Vice President
Functional executive. Member of the leadership team. Reports to the CEO or to a C-level executive.

## 4. Level and Tenure Are Different Things

### 4.1 Common Confusion
Several policies in this handbook grant entitlements by **tenure tier** (a function of length of service, defined in HR-001 §4), and others grant entitlements by **job level** (defined here). These are independent. A newly hired IC6 is in Tenure Tier A; an IC2 with seven years of service is in Tenure Tier C.

### 4.2 Which Policies Use Which
Policies keyed to **tenure tier**: paid time off accrual (HR-004 §3), sabbatical eligibility (HR-004 §8), and the employer pension contribution schedule for Ireland (HR-003 §5.2).

Policies keyed to **job level**: salary band (HR-002 §3), bonus target (HR-002 §5), long-haul cabin class (FIN-001 §4.2), learning and development budget (HR-006 §3), and notice period on resignation (HR-007 §2.1).

Policies keyed to **neither**: per diem rates, which are keyed to destination city tier (FIN-001 §5), and equipment standards, which are keyed to job family (IT-001 §3).

## 5. Levelling New Hires

### 5.1 Process
Hiring managers propose a level; the recruiter validates it against the band; People Operations approves. Offers at IC6/M3 and above additionally require executive sponsor approval.

### 5.2 Down-levelling and Up-levelling in Process
A candidate may be offered a level different from the one advertised. Where the offered level is below the advertised level, the recruiter must disclose this to the candidate before the offer is verbal.

### 5.3 No Level Guarantees
An offer letter states a level and a salary. Neither constitutes a promise of future promotion. See HR-005 §6 for how promotions actually work.
