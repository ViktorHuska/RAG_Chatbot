# HR-007 — Resignation, Termination, and Offboarding

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Scope
This document covers notice periods, the offboarding process, final pay, equipment return, access revocation, equity treatment, and post-employment obligations. It applies to employees of all Meridian legal entities. Contractor disengagement is governed by the applicable statement of work, not by this document, though the access and equipment provisions in §6 and §7 apply to contractors as well.

## 2. Resignation

### 2.1 Notice Periods
The notice period an employee is expected to give on resignation is keyed to **job level** (ORG-002 §3), subject to any longer period stated in the individual employment contract, which prevails.

| Level | Expected notice |
| --- | --- |
| IC1 – IC5 / M1 – M2 | 2 weeks |
| IC6 – IC7 / M3 and above | 4 weeks |

Employees of the Irish and German entities are subject to statutory minimum notice periods, which increase with length of service and which override the table above where longer. Employees should check their contract; People Operations can confirm the applicable period.

### 2.2 How to Resign
Notice is given in writing to the manager of record, copying People Operations. The effective last day is agreed in writing. Meridian may, at its discretion, release an employee before the end of the notice period, in which case pay continues to the agreed last day.

### 2.3 Garden Leave
For roles with access to competitively sensitive information, Meridian may place a resigning employee on garden leave for all or part of the notice period. Pay and benefits continue; system access is revoked (§6.1); the employee remains employed and bound by all obligations of employment.

### 2.4 Withdrawal of Resignation
A resignation may be withdrawn only with the written agreement of the manager and People Operations, and only before the role has been advertised or backfilled.

## 3. Termination by Meridian

### 3.1 United States
U.S. employment is at-will and may be ended by either party at any time. Where termination follows a performance improvement plan (HR-005 §5.3), the plan record forms part of the file. People Operations must be involved before any termination decision is communicated.

### 3.2 Ireland, Germany, Canada, and Singapore
Termination follows the statutory process applicable in the jurisdiction, including any required consultation, notice, and, in Germany, works council involvement. No termination in these entities proceeds without prior legal review.

### 3.3 Severance
Severance is not a standard entitlement in the U.S. and is offered at the company's discretion, typically in exchange for a separation agreement. In other entities, statutory severance applies where the law requires it.

### 3.4 Redundancy
Where a role is eliminated, the applicable statutory selection and consultation process is followed. Affected employees receive priority consideration for internal roles for 60 days and are exempt from the 12-month-in-role requirement in HR-005 §7.3.

## 4. Final Pay

### 4.1 Timing
Final pay is issued on the next scheduled pay date after the last day of employment, or earlier where local law requires. Several U.S. states require final pay on the last day for involuntary terminations; Payroll applies the stricter of local law and this policy.

### 4.2 Paid Time Off
Accrued and unused paid time off is paid out under HR-004 §4.4. Floating holidays and expired carryover are not paid out. Unused sick leave (HR-004 §5) is not paid out. An unused sabbatical entitlement (HR-004 §8) has no cash value.

### 4.3 Deductions
Outstanding obligations are deducted from final pay where local law permits and where the employee has given any required written consent. These include: negative PTO balances (HR-004 §3.6), relocation repayment (HR-002 §8.4), study agreement repayment (HR-006 §6.2), unreturned equipment charges (§7.4), and outstanding expense advances.

### 4.4 Bonus
An employee must be employed and not under notice on the bonus payout date to receive a bonus (HR-002 §5.2). An employee who resigns in February, before the March payout, does not receive the prior year's bonus, except where local law provides otherwise.

## 5. Equity

### 5.1 Vesting Stops
Vesting stops on the last day of employment. There is no vesting during any post-employment period.

### 5.2 Exercise Windows
Treatment of vested awards is governed by the equity plan documents and the individual grant agreement, not by this handbook. Employees should request a personalized statement from Finance before their last day, as some communications become harder to route after access is revoked.

## 6. Access Revocation

### 6.1 Timing
All system access — email, Slack, cloud infrastructure, code repositories, customer systems, the password manager — is revoked at the end of the last working day, or immediately on the commencement of garden leave (§2.3), or immediately on involuntary termination.

### 6.2 Restricted Data Access
Access to Restricted-classified data (SEC-001 §3.4) is revoked as soon as notice is given, regardless of the remaining notice period. This is not a judgment about the individual; it is a standing control.

### 6.3 Personal Data
Employees may not copy, forward, or retain Meridian data of any classification, including their own work product. Personal files stored on company systems should be removed before the last day. Meridian does not provide data exports of mailboxes or drives to departing employees.

### 6.4 Confirmation
The manager confirms in the offboarding checklist that no shared credentials or automation the employee owned remain in use. Service accounts and scheduled jobs owned by the departing employee must be reassigned before the last day.

## 7. Equipment Return

### 7.1 What Must Be Returned
All Meridian-owned equipment: laptop, monitors, docking stations, peripherals purchased under IT-001 §4, security keys, mobile devices issued under IT-001 §3.4, and any item purchased under HR-006 §5.2 above USD 500.

### 7.2 Deadline
Equipment must be returned within **10 business days** of the last working day. Remote employees receive a prepaid shipping label from IT; the same 10-business-day deadline applies from the date the label is issued.

### 7.3 Condition
Normal wear is expected and is not charged. Equipment must be returned with any Meridian asset tag intact and must not be wiped or factory-reset by the employee — IT performs the wipe, which is part of the data-handling chain of custody under SEC-001 §6.

### 7.4 Non-Return
Equipment not returned within 30 calendar days of the last working day is charged at depreciated replacement value and deducted from final pay where permitted, or invoiced where not. Depreciation follows the refresh schedule in IT-001 §3.2.

## 8. Post-Employment Obligations

### 8.1 Confidentiality
Confidentiality obligations survive employment indefinitely for trade secrets and for the term stated in the employment agreement for other confidential information.

### 8.2 Non-Solicitation
Where a non-solicitation covenant applies, its terms are in the employment agreement. Meridian does not apply post-employment non-competition covenants to U.S. employees.

### 8.3 References
Meridian confirms dates of employment, job title, and location. Substantive references are given at the discretion of the individual manager and are personal to them, not statements by Meridian.

### 8.4 Rehire
Former employees are eligible for rehire unless the file records otherwise. A rehire starts a new continuous-service clock for all handbook purposes (HR-001 §4.3) — tenure tier resets to Tier A, and sabbatical eligibility (HR-004 §8.3) restarts.

## 9. Offboarding Interview

### 9.1 Voluntary
People Operations offers an offboarding interview to every departing employee. Participation is voluntary. Themes are aggregated quarterly for the leadership team; individual comments are not attributed without the employee's consent.
