# HR-001 — Employment Classification, Probation, and Tenure

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Scope
This document defines the categories of worker engaged by Meridian Systems, the probationary period applicable to each, and the **tenure tier** definition used throughout this handbook to grant length-of-service entitlements. It applies to all Meridian legal entities.

### 1.2 Why This Document Matters to Other Policies
Tenure tiers defined in §4 of this document are referenced by HR-004 (paid time off), HR-003 (pension contributions in Ireland), and HR-004 §8 (sabbatical). If you are trying to work out how much leave you accrue, you must first establish your tenure tier here.

## 2. Worker Categories

### 2.1 Regular Full-Time Employee
An employee engaged for an indefinite term who is regularly scheduled for 30 or more hours per week. Regular full-time employees are eligible for the full range of Meridian benefits described in HR-003, subject to any waiting periods stated there.

### 2.2 Regular Part-Time Employee
An employee engaged for an indefinite term who is regularly scheduled for at least 20 but fewer than 30 hours per week. Regular part-time employees accrue paid time off on a pro-rata basis by scheduled hours (HR-004 §3.4) and are eligible for benefits as set out in HR-003 §2.2. An employee scheduled for fewer than 20 hours per week is not a regular part-time employee and is engaged under §2.3 or §2.5.

### 2.3 Fixed-Term Employee
An employee engaged for a defined term or a defined project, stated in the offer letter. Fixed-term employees receive the same benefits as regular employees of the same scheduled hours, except that they are not eligible for the sabbatical program (HR-004 §8) or for equity grants. In Ireland and Germany, statutory protections applying to fixed-term workers apply in full and take precedence over this document.

### 2.4 Intern
A student or recent graduate engaged for a term of 8 to 16 weeks through the Meridian internship program. Interns are paid, are not eligible for benefits other than those required by law and the wellness stipend (HR-003 §7), and do not accrue paid time off. Interns receive paid company holidays.

### 2.5 Contractor
An individual engaged through a staffing agency or as an independent business entity. **Contractors are not Meridian employees.** They are not covered by HR-002 through HR-007 and are not eligible for Meridian benefits, paid time off, bonus, or equity. Contractors are subject to SEC-001 in full, and to the contractor-specific provisions of IT-001 §5, which differ substantially from the equipment provisions applying to employees.

Engagement of a contractor requires an approved statement of work and Finance countersignature. Managers must not direct contractor work in a manner inconsistent with the agency agreement; questions go to People Operations before, not after.

### 2.6 Exempt and Non-Exempt (United States Only)
U.S. employees are classified as exempt or non-exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Non-exempt employees must record all hours worked and are paid overtime at 1.5× the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. This classification has a specific interaction with on-call compensation described in ENG-001 §5.2. Classification is determined by People Operations based on duties, not by title or by employee preference.

## 3. Probationary Period

### 3.1 United States and Canada
New regular employees serve a 90-calendar-day introductory period. Completion is not automatic; the manager confirms completion in Workday. U.S. employment remains at-will before, during, and after this period.

### 3.2 Ireland and Germany
New regular employees serve a probationary period of six months, extendable once by a further three months where local law permits and where the extension is agreed in writing before the initial period ends. Notice periods during probation are shorter than the standard periods in HR-007 §2 and are stated in the individual contract.

### 3.3 Singapore
New regular employees serve a three-month probationary period.

### 3.4 Effect on Benefits
Probation does not delay medical, dental, or vision enrolment, which begin on the first of the month following the start date (HR-003 §3.1). It does delay eligibility for the referral bonus (HR-002 §7) and for internal transfer (HR-005 §7.3).

## 4. Tenure Tiers

### 4.1 Definition
Tenure tier is a function of **continuous service** with any Meridian legal entity, measured from the most recent hire date, expressed in completed months.

| Tier | Continuous service | Common description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier A | 0 through 24 completed months | First two years |
| Tier B | 25 through 60 completed months | Years three through five |
| Tier C | 61 or more completed months | Six years and beyond |

### 4.2 When a Tier Change Takes Effect
A tier change takes effect on the **first day of the month following** the month in which the service threshold is met. An employee whose 25th month of service completes on 14 June moves to Tier B on 1 July.

### 4.3 Continuous Service and Breaks
Service is continuous across transfers between Meridian legal entities, across changes of worker category under §2, and across approved leaves of absence including parental leave. Service is **not** continuous across a termination and rehire: a rehired employee starts a new service clock at their rehire date and returns to Tier A, regardless of prior service. Time engaged as a contractor under §2.5 does not count toward continuous service, even if the contractor later converts to employment.

### 4.4 Tenure Tier Is Not Job Level
See ORG-002 §4 for the distinction. Tenure tiers do not affect salary band, bonus target, or travel entitlements.

## 5. Changes to Classification

### 5.1 Requesting a Change
A change between full-time and part-time status requires manager approval and 30 days' notice, and is processed effective the first day of a pay period. A reduction below 20 hours per week is not available as a permanent arrangement; employees seeking that should discuss a fixed-term or leave arrangement with People Operations.

### 5.2 Effect on Accrued Entitlements
A change from full-time to part-time does not forfeit already-accrued paid time off; the accrual **rate** changes prospectively (HR-004 §3.4). A change does not reset tenure tier.

### 5.3 Contractor Conversion
Conversion of a contractor to employee status is treated as a new hire for all purposes in this handbook, including probation (§3), tenure tier (§4.3), and benefits waiting periods (HR-003).
